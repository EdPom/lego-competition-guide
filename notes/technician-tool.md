# Technician tool: strategy-design assumptions

[Home](../README.md) · [Official resource index](../INDEX.md)

Recorded September 21, 2026 for BIOGLOW Future Edition, grades 3–8. Use alongside the [driving-base reference](driving-base.md) and [attachment reference](driving-base-attachments.md). Explicit rules and working interpretations are distinguished below; these are planning notes, not approval of a particular design. Page numbers refer to PDF pages.

## Baseline

The Technician tool is a robotic build including a single motor and a color sensor. The example launcher illustrates one approach; rule 14 explicitly allows teams to change example designs and code. Rule 15 explicitly permits the driving base to move this tool outside the Technician area to complete missions. We can therefore explore controlled delivery using a carried tool as well as launching from the starting area.

## Explicit rules and their design consequences

| Topic | Baseline | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Hardware | Include the permitted single motor and color sensor. The other single motor and sensor are required in the hive and young forest. No extra electronics allowance is created by changing the tool's design. | Rules 5, 8, 17; pp. 4–5, 10. |
| Quantity | The hardware budget supports one such powered tool at a time. No separate numerical quota for passive LEGO attachments is stated; spare builds must fit the placement/storage rules and cannot add motors or sensors. | Rules 4–5, 8–9, 13, 17. |
| Control | Direct motor input, triggering by a color sensor, or autonomous programming. There is no third handheld-controller allocation for the Technician. | Rules 12 and 17; pp. 6, 10. |
| Specialist programming | The Specialist may operate other player tools through a program on the team laptop/tablet. | Rule 18; p. 11. |
| Start | The tool starts in the Technician area. Its connection cards also start there. | Rule 9 and Technician Role Card p. 2. |
| Size | Equipment must be no taller than **8 inches / 203 mm during inspection** and fit its designated starting area. No separate numerical length/width limit is stated. | Rule 9; p. 5. |
| Materials | Non-electric LEGO pieces may come from any set, in any quantity. No glue, painting, or modification except cutting LEGO string/tubing; no wind-up motors, pneumatics, or extra game models. | Rule 4; p. 3. |
| Hand loading/handling | The universal rule permits handling only items completely inside the player's area unless an exception applies. The example explicitly demonstrates loading a token into the tool inside the Technician area. | Rule 14; Technician Role Card p. 2. |
| Sending items out | Items may leave the Technician area only using the robotic tool or driving base. Hand passing, pushing, or throwing between players is prohibited. | Rules 14 and 17. |
| Carried operation | The driving base may transport the robotic tool outside the Technician area for mission work. The player's hand permissions do not travel with it. | Rules 14–15. |
| Changing the car | The Technician may modify the driving base/change attachments when the base is completely inside their area. | Rule 17; p. 10. |

The team has one required field connection card and up to three additional cards for match use (rule 6), and at most one laptop/tablet (rule 7). These are shared team allowances, not separate allowances for each tool.

## Loading tokens by hand

The Technician may pick up tokens completely inside the Technician area and load the robotic tool there. The Technician Role Card p. 2 explicitly demonstrates loading one keystone token into the example launcher.

Rules 14 and 17 also support hand-loading a driving-base attachment or carried container when the complete base, attachment, and tokens are inside the Technician area. This is an application of the general handling rules, rather than a separately illustrated loading example. A return, reload, and redeploy sequence can therefore be considered in strategy design.

Hand access does not extend to a token partly outside the area or a tool deployed on the field. Items leave this area through the robotic tool or driving base, rather than a hand push, throw, or pass.

## Boundary and control assumptions to preserve

**Tool movement and hand access are different permissions.** The Technician tool is not subject to the Operator rule requiring tools to stay entirely in the player area. Nevertheless, the universal handling rule still applies. Do not assume the Technician can hold a long tool extending into the field, reload a deployed tool by hand, or reach out to adjust it merely because its base or handle remains inside the area. Our supported mobile route is to deploy it using the driving base and use an allowed control method.

**Mounting does not transfer ownership of the controls.** A Driver-controller button directly operating the Technician tool is not explicitly authorized by the mounting/transport permission. For every mobile design, specify what triggers its action outside the area and how that follows rule 17 or the Specialist's programming exception. Physical motor input by the Technician still requires permitted hand access.

**There is no general hand-retrieval exception for any stranded tool.** Rule 19 leaves uncontrolled equipment where it rests unless moved through normal gameplay. Rule 15's stuck-driving-base courtesy procedure is specific to that situation; it is not permission to reach out and rescue any deployed mechanism.

## Working interpretations for strategy design

A dispenser, gate, or lift using the permitted single motor and sensor is a candidate alternative to the example launcher. Mounting it on the driving base is a working interpretation of the explicit transport permission. A specific design still needs compliant controls, starting placement, handling, and mission contact.

For a sketch, record: what the motor does; where the sensor sits and whether/how it triggers the action; where loading happens; how the tool reaches its target; what initiates delivery; whether the token finishes loose where required; and how the tool returns or recovers from failure. Preserve both required tool components even when the chosen program does not use sensor triggering.
