# Driving-base attachments: strategy-design assumptions

[Home](../README.md) · [Official resource index](../INDEX.md)

Recorded September 21, 2026 for BIOGLOW Future Edition, grades 3–8. Use this as the equipment baseline when comparing the children's strategy ideas. **Explicit rules** below are separated from **working interpretations**; this document is not approval of a particular build. Page numbers refer to PDF pages.

## Baseline

Companion references: [driving base](driving-base.md) and [Technician tool](technician-tool.md) cover the complete tools, including deployment, servicing, hand-access boundaries, and recovery.

We can explore both passive LEGO attachments and a driving base carrying the Technician's motorized tool. The supplied scoop and launcher are example designs, not required designs. Carrying the robotic tool is explicitly supported; integrating it as a mounted dispenser or lift is our design interpretation and must preserve its hardware and control rules.

## Electrical budget

The required field hardware is included in the team totals; it is not an additional allowance.

| Hardware | Team total | Required field use | Available for team tools |
|---|---:|---|---|
| Double motor | 2 | 1 in grand tree | 1 for driving base |
| Single motor | 2 | 1 in hive | 1 for Technician robotic tool |
| Color sensor | 2 | 1 in young forest | 1 for Technician robotic tool |
| Controller | 2 | None supplied as required field hardware | Up to 1 Driver controller and 1 Specialist controller; no additional tool controller |

Only the specified hardware types are permitted. Consequently, **the car may carry its double motor plus the Technician tool's single motor and color sensor**. That does not authorize another double motor, extra single motors/sensors, or moving required field hardware onto the car. Sources: rules 5, 8, 12, 15, and 17.

## Mounting does not change control permissions

- The driving base may use the Driver controller or run autonomously (rule 15).
- The Technician robotic tool uses direct motor input, a color-sensor trigger, or autonomous programming (rule 17). Physical handling remains subject to area restrictions.
- The Specialist may operate other player tools through a program on their laptop/tablet (rule 18).
- Players generally cannot operate another player's tool without an explicit exception (rule 14). **Do not assume a Driver-controller button can directly operate the Technician tool merely because it is mounted on the car.** Record the proposed trigger/control path when designing it.

Rule 12's restriction on grips/guards performing game actions concerns attachments to the **handheld controllers**, not a general ban on mission-performing attachments to the driving base.

## How to use this when proposing strategies

A passive scoop, funnel, carrier, or contact-operated gate is a candidate under the general equipment rules. A powered dispenser or lift is a candidate using the carried Technician tool's existing single motor and sensor. These are design options to investigate, not tested mechanisms or blanket rulings.

For each candidate sketch, record: its inspection/start footprint and height; its hardware; who loads or changes it and where; what triggers it after leaving the Technician area; and how it releases tokens into the required scoring condition. Mark any departure from the explicit rules above as an interpretation to resolve against the concrete design.
