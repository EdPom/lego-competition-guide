# Driving base: strategy-design assumptions

[Home](../README.md) · [Official resource index](../INDEX.md)

Recorded September 21, 2026 for BIOGLOW Future Edition, grades 3–8. Use alongside the [attachment reference](driving-base-attachments.md) and [Technician-tool reference](technician-tool.md). Explicit rules and working interpretations are distinguished below; these are planning notes, not approval of a particular design. Page numbers refer to PDF pages.

## Baseline

The Driver tool consists of a controller and a driving base containing a double motor. The base can navigate, move tokens, and transport the Technician's robotic tool outside the Technician area to complete missions. The supplied scoop car is an example, not a required chassis or a restriction to the haven mission. Sources: rule 15, the Driver Role Card, and the example-design guidance under rule 14.

## Explicit rules and their design consequences

| Topic | Baseline | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Hardware | One double motor remains for the base after the required grand-tree motor is allocated. The Driver may use one controller. Only the specified hardware types are allowed. | Rules 5, 8, 12, 15; pp. 4–6, 8. |
| Other electronics aboard | The base may transport the Technician tool, which includes the remaining single motor and color sensor. Carrying that tool does not increase the team's hardware allowance. | Rules 5, 8, 15, 17. |
| Control | Driver-controller operation or autonomous operation is allowed. The Specialist may also operate player tools through a program on the team's laptop/tablet. | Rules 15 and 18; pp. 8, 11. |
| Starting position | The base starts completely inside the Technician area; connection cards used for it or the robotic tool also start there. | Rule 9 and Driver Role Card p. 2. |
| Size | Equipment height is limited to **8 inches / 203 mm during inspection**. No separate numerical length/width limit is stated; fit the starting equipment in its designated area. | Rule 9; p. 5. |
| Materials and quantity | Non-electric LEGO pieces are unrestricted in quantity and set origin. No glue, paint, or part modification, except cutting LEGO string/tubing. Wind-up motors, pneumatics, and extra game models are prohibited. | Rule 4; p. 3. |
| Driver's hands | The Driver handles only their controller, not the base, attachments, tokens, or mission models. | Rule 15; p. 8. |
| Servicing the base | The Technician may modify the base and change attachments when it is completely inside the Technician area. | Rule 17; p. 10. |
| Leaving the area | The base can carry items out of the Technician area. Players cannot substitute a hand pass, push, or throw between areas. | Rules 14 and 17. |
| Field conduct | Do not disassemble/modify mission models or negatively interfere with the other team's movement, access, or scoring. | Rules 14 and 20. |

See the [shared equipment details](driving-base-attachments.md#size-quantity-materials-and-handling) for storage, expansion, and attachment limits. The absence of an attachment-count limit is not an unlimited allowance for additional powered vehicles: our baseline is the one driving base supported by the available double motor.

## If the base gets stuck

Rule 15 provides a specific recovery procedure:

1. Notify the referee.
2. The Technician may return the base to the Technician area.
3. Items carried by the base are handed to the referee and removed from play; items merely pushed remain where they are.

Strategic misuse can incur a penalty. This is not a free reset or a way to transport a load back home. For design comparisons, consider how a failed run affects both the base and its carried items. The Driver still does not pick it up.

## Working interpretations for strategy design

- A custom chassis, passive scoop, guide, carrier, or mechanically actuated attachment is a candidate under the general equipment rules; the example car need not be copied.
- A mounted dispenser or lift can be explored as the carried Technician tool. Its control and handling restrictions remain those of that tool; attachment to the car does not grant the Driver unrestricted control of it.
- A folding or extending design may be worth exploring because the stated height restriction applies during inspection. No separate operating-height limit was found, but the other placement, handling, storage, and interference rules still apply.

For a candidate sketch, record its starting footprint, inspection height, hardware, load, control method, return/service plan, and the exact final token placement it is intended to achieve. Test collection reliability and travel time separately from rule compliance.
